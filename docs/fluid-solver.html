Fluid solver

ArticleReference

From Warp Wiki, the reference for the Warp engine SDK

GPU stable-fluids solver with obstacles, dye, and budgets

Fluid solver
CategoryWorld and simulation
Modulewarp_sdk::fluid::solver
Sourcewarp_sdk/src/fluid/solver.rs
Public items28
Examplecargo run -p warp_sdk --example fluid

Fluid solver is a stable-fluids simulation that runs on wgpu compute shaders. It is the type Solver in warp_sdk::fluid::solver. The solver keeps a velocity field and up to 16 dye channels on a grid that covers one world rect, and it publishes the result as textures.[1] Most games reach it through Ocean, which makes and steps a solver for them.

Overview

Each fixed substep runs these compute passes.[2]

  1. Advection of the velocity, by the semi-Lagrangian or the MacCormack method. The same pass adds the fade, the velocity splats and the obstacles.
  2. Vorticity confinement: a curl pass, then a force pass.
  3. Divergence, a warm-started pressure solve, then the projection with free-slip walls and obstacles.
  4. Advection of the dye with the projected velocity, with the dye splats.

All the work goes into the encoder of the game, and the CPU never reads the fields back. The module exists because a CPU fluid proved too slow in an earlier game. The commit that added it records that history.

Usage

The module header shows one frame. Solver::new takes the device as an Arc, because the solver keeps a clone: Solver::set_obstacles makes a bind group later.[3]

use warp_sdk::fluid::solver::{Solver, SolverConfig};
// gpu.device is an Arc<wgpu::Device>, for example from SharedGpu.
let mut fluid = Solver::new(&gpu.device, SolverConfig::medium([-8.0, -4.5, 8.0, 4.5]))?;
fluid.splat([0.0, 0.0], [2.0, 0.0], 0.4);       // push the water
fluid.splat_dye(0, [0.0, 0.0], 1.0, 0.4);       // and give it colour
fluid.step(&gpu.device, &gpu.queue, &mut encoder, dt);
let vel = fluid.velocity_view();                // Rgba16Float, .xy = world units per second
if fluid.over_budget() { /* make the next solver with a lower Quality */ }

Submit the encoder before the next Solver::step.[4]

Configuration

SolverConfig holds all the settings. Start from a preset and change fields. SolverConfig::preset takes a Quality, and SolverConfig::low, SolverConfig::medium and SolverConfig::high are short forms. default() for SolverConfig is medium over the world rect [-1, -1, 1, 1].

Quality Cells on the long side Pressure iterations MacCormack
Quality::Low 128 16 no
Quality::Medium 256 24 no
Quality::High 512 32 yes

The long side of the world rect gets the preset cell count. The short side gets the count that keeps the cells square, clamped to at least 8.[5] A rect of 32 x 18 at Quality::Medium is 256 x 144 cells.

All presets share the other values.

Field Preset value Meaning
dye_channels 4 Dye channels, 0 to 16
pressure_solver PressureSolver::RedBlack The pressure method
pressure_warm_start 0.9 The part of the last pressure that starts the next solve
vorticity 1.5 Confinement strength, 0 turns it off
velocity_dissipation 0.2 Velocity loss per second
dye_dissipation 0.3 Dye loss per second
fixed_dt 1/60 The fixed time step in seconds
max_substeps 2 The most substeps in one Solver::step
max_splats 64 The most splats between two substeps
gpu_budget_ms 1.0 The GPU time that one step may use

Solver::set_vorticity changes the strength after creation.

How it works

Fixed substeps

Solver::step takes the frame time and runs a fixed step. A non-finite dt counts as 0, and a larger one clamps to 0.25 s. The time goes into an accumulator, and the call runs at most max_substeps substeps. The solver drops the rest of the frame time, so the fluid slows down but the frame does not.[6] A slow frame thus cannot make the next frame slower. time_dropped reports the loss.

If the accumulator holds less than one fixed step, the call runs no substep and copies nothing. Queued splats wait for the next substep.

Grid and textures

One texel is one cell. Texel (0, 0) is the cell at the world rect minimum, and +y in texels is +y in the world.[7] Cell centres are at texel coordinate i + 0.5. The velocity and dye textures are Rgba16Float, and the scalar fields (solid, curl, divergence and pressure) are R32Float.

The solver ping-pongs between pairs of textures, and it makes every bind group once in Solver::new. After the substeps, it copies the newest fields into separate output textures. The views of those outputs do not change during the life of the solver, so a game can keep a bind group that uses them.[8]

Solver::velocity_view gives the velocity, with .xy in world units per second. Dye channel c is component c % 4 of dye_view(c / 4), and Solver::dye_groups gives the number of dye textures.

Advection

The semi-Lagrangian pass reads each field at the point that the cell velocity traces back to, with a bilinear read built from textureLoad. This pass also applies the fade and the splats and clears solid cells.[9]

MacCormack keeps more detail and costs two more passes for each field. It runs a forward step, a backward step, and a correction hat + 0.5 * (phi - bar). The result is clamped to the four cells at the back position, and the clamp keeps it stable.[10]

Splats

Solver::splat adds velocity around a world point at the next substep, with a Gaussian of world radius. Solver::splat_dye does the same for one dye channel, and a channel that does not exist does nothing. A splat with a value that is not finite is dropped, and a splat past max_splats is dropped too. splats_dropped counts them. Call Solver::splat each frame while a body pushes the water.

fluid.splat(boat.pos, boat.vel, 0.6);

The splats apply in the first substep of a step only. The second substep gets a splat count of 0.[11]

Vorticity confinement

The curl pass measures the rotation of each cell, and the force pass pushes along the gradient of its magnitude, scaled by vorticity and the cell size.[12]

Pressure projection

The projection subtracts the pressure gradient from the velocity to remove divergence. PressureSolver::Jacobi uses one pass per iteration. PressureSolver::RedBlack uses two passes, red cells then black cells, and it converges about two times faster than Jacobi for the same number of passes.[13]

Each solve starts from pressure_warm_start times the last pressure, so few iterations give a good result. Because storage textures here are write-only, each red-black pass also copies the cells that it does not update.

A wall or obstacle cell has zero velocity, and its pressure is the pressure of the fluid cell beside it, which gives a zero normal gradient.[14]

Obstacles

Solver::set_obstacles takes a signed distance texture and its own world rect [min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y]. The texture is R16Float or R32Float, in world units, negative inside a solid, at any resolution. Cells outside the rect are open water.[15]

The solver resamples the texture into a grid-resolution solid field once per Solver::step, before the substeps. A game can therefore draw moving obstacles into the same texture each frame. Solver::clear_obstacles removes them, and only the grid walls stay.

Walls are free-slip: water slides along them but does not go in. In project, a cell within two cells of an obstacle loses the part of its velocity that goes into the obstacle, using a normal from the distance gradient. The grid edges apply the same rule on each axis.[16]

Terrain gives its rect as origin and size, not as min and max. Convert it with size_rect_to_bounds.

let (view, rect) = terrain.sdf_texture(&device, &queue);
solver.set_obstacles(view, size_rect_to_bounds(rect));

Metrics

Solver::stats returns SolverStats on every device: the substeps, the dispatches, the dropped time and splats, and the CPU record time.

GPU time needs the TIMESTAMP_QUERY feature. Two timestamps surround each step, and a ring of three buffers reads them back, so the CPU never waits for the GPU.[17] Solver::last_gpu_ms is None with no such feature, and its result comes two or three frames late. Solver::over_budget compares a smoothed time with gpu_budget_ms, and it is always false with no timestamps. The solver does not lower its own quality. The game makes a new solver with a lower Quality.

Errors

Solver::new returns a SolverError.

  • SolverError::NoCompute means that the device has no compute shaders or too few storage textures, for example a GLES or WebGL2 device. The check asks for a workgroup of 8 x 8, at least 2 storage textures, 1 storage buffer and 5 sampled textures per stage.
  • SolverError::BadConfig means a value out of range: a grid under 2 x 2 or over the device limit, a world rect with no area, more than MAX_DYE_CHANNELS dye channels, a fixed_dt that is not positive, or max_substeps of 0.
match Solver::new(&device, cfg) {
    Ok(s) => Some(s),
    Err(SolverError::NoCompute(why)) => { eprintln!("no GPU fluid: {why}"); None }
    Err(e) => panic!("{e}"),
}

Limitations

  • The velocity and pressure live at cell centres on one grid. The commit that added the solver states that this collocated grid reduces divergence but does not remove all of it. The test projection_removes_most_divergence asks for a fall to under 35% of the start value.
  • A resolution change needs a new Solver.
  • The solver has no public read back of its fields.
  • The header target of under 1 ms for Medium applies to a mid-range GPU.[18]

History

Commit d905e000 added the module on 2026-10-29. A measurement in that commit gives a Medium step as 0.15 ms of GPU time on an RTX 4060 Ti.

#pub fn set_obstacles(&mut self, sdf: &wgpu::TextureView, sdf_rect: [f32; 4])

Use a signed distance texture as the obstacles. The texture is R16Float or R32Float, in world units, NEGATIVE inside a solid. It can have any resolution, and sdf_rect is its own world rect [min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y]. Cells outside sdf_rect are open water.

#pub fn step(&mut self, device: &wgpu::Device, queue: &wgpu::Queue, encoder: &mut wgpu::CommandEncoder, dt: f32)

Advance the fluid by the frame time dt. The solver runs zero or more fixed substeps (at most max_substeps) into encoder, then copies the result into the views. Submit the encoder before the next step.
